Slaughterhouse Optimization

Paula Operations Manual

Cairo 3A · Module 4 — the dual-mode optimization engine that decides how many birds of which weight to slaughter to maximise profit. The cockpit is where planners launch and approve runs, sales teams submit demand and prices, the sales director reviews them, and everyone reads the plan, the market forecast and how the models performed — each person signed in with their own account and role.

  • 13 screens + sign-in
  • 6 roles
  • Desktop ≥ 1280 px
  • EGP · kg · tonnes

Since the last manual

What's new in this release

Paula has grown from a planning cockpit into a shared workspace for planners, sales teams and the sales director. If you knew the August manual, here is what changed.

  1. Personal accounts and roles. Everyone now signs in with their own email. What you see depends on your role, and the Admin's sidebar is grouped into Runs, Demand, Commercial, Run results and Analysis. Jump to… (⌘K / Ctrl K) finds any page you can open.
  2. Commercial inputs. A new section where sales teams submit demand and prices each cycle, and the sales director adjusts, approves and turns them into a demand set. It includes Discounts & rebates and Sales teams.
  3. Demand sets. Demand is now versioned, shareable and comparable, and a run can pick a saved set, not only a new upload or the model forecast.
  4. Three kinds of supply. The Planning form can run on the Weight forecast, a Saved harvest plan or Manual supply, with frozen storage dates and an Allocate SKUs switch.
  5. Approve and share a plan. One run per period can be marked as the approved plan. Runs are private to their creator until shared.
  6. Deeper run results. A Temperature tab explains each fresh-or-frozen decision, a Demand uplift tab shows what could recover a shortfall, and coverage chips explain why a category fell short.
  7. SKU Allocation and Frozen recommendations are back, both rebuilt. SKU Allocation is a configurable table for one run. Frozen recommendations says what to sell, part-sell or hold, to whom, and what waiting costs.
  8. Market Intelligence and Evaluation grew. Market adds SKU Factors and frozen views. Evaluation now scores nine models across Price factors, Demand and Weight, one month at a time.
  9. Settings can change things. Admins edit parameters with a reason and a version history, manage cutting options, SKU & customer mapping, and Users & Roles.
  10. Money is no longer absent from run results. Most of the page still uses factors and indices, but the Production plan now shows an average direct cost in EGP per bird, and the Demand uplift tab shows profit estimates to Admins and Planners.

Demand uplift tab: what could recover a shortfall (demand runs only)

1
2
3
  1. Why uplift A short category is often held back because the cutting option that makes it also makes a co-product nobody ordered. Uplifting that co-product lets the option run.
  2. Generate demand uplift Finds the blocking co-product for each short category. Next, pick a route per category, edit the tonnes, then Create uplifted demand set and Run plan on uplifted demand.
  3. Regenerate & compare Regenerate refreshes the routes. Once the uplifted run exists, the tab becomes a two-column comparison with Compare all three plans: Production, Demand and Uplifted side by side.

Creating an uplifted set is a commitment to sell more, so the confirmation says so; nothing runs at that step and the routes then lock. Admins and Planners also see estimated profit in EGP here. Other roles read the tab, but the create and run buttons are replaced by a sentence naming the role they need.

Warnings, coverage, approval and sharing

  1. Plan warnings. A banner above the tabs lists what degraded the plan without failing it. It cannot be dismissed.
  2. Coverage chips. On Production's category table and Per-day's monthly orders, a chip names why a category fell short: Blocked by a co-product, Cap, Supply limit, No active option, Outside window, or Mixed. Click it for the routes to recover the shortfall.
  3. Approve plan (Admin, Planner). Makes a completed run the plan for its period. Only one run per period can hold the mark; if another does, the dialog says so and links to it.
  4. Share run (Admin, Planner). Pick a colleague and Can view or Can view and re-run. They get a notification, and cannot delete or re-share the run.

While a run executes, this page shows live progress with a day counter, and the results replace it when the run lands. A failed or cancelled run says so explicitly. Older runs may lack newer panels; for example, "No temperature decisions recorded" means they were not recorded, not that none were made.

Yield, Price Factor & Cost Factor per Cut

For any cutting option a run chose, you can see what each cut yielded, what it sells for, and what it costs, relative to the live-bird price. Open a run, go to the Production tab, scroll to Production plan and expand an option.

1
2
3
4
5
6
  1. Cutting option Ranked by contribution index. Each row shows produced tonnes, overall yield, contribution index, average direct cost and the categories it produced. Click the arrow to expand it.
  2. Yield The share of live weight that becomes this cut. A dash means no yield applies to that row.
  3. Price factor The cut's selling price as a multiple of the live-bird price. 1.80 means 1.8 × the live-bird price.
  4. Cost factor The cut's cost on the same scale, cost ÷ live-bird price, so it compares directly with the price factor.
  5. Profit index Price factor minus cost factor: the margin per unit of live-bird price, comparable across cuts. Contribution index follows in the next column.
  6. Direct cost, avg (EGP/bird) The option's average direct cost per bird, in EGP. Shown to economics roles when the run carries it; the info icon gives the breakdown.

How to read a row

From the screenshot: Drumsticks under this option made 4.73 t at a 12.0% yield, with a price factor of 1.80 and a cost factor of 1.22, so the profit index is 1.80 − 1.22 = 0.58. Fillet sells higher (4.11) and costs more (2.64), and still earns more per unit at 1.47. To turn a factor into money, multiply by the live-bird price: at an illustrative 70 EGP/kg, a factor of 1.80 is 126 EGP/kg.

Commercial

Commercial Cycles

Each cycle is one submission window. Sales teams enter demand and prices per channel, customer and SKU, and the sales director reviews, adjusts and approves them. Approval creates a demand set that planners can run against.

  1. Open. The director presses New cycle and sets the window start and a length of 1–4 weeks.
  2. Teams submit. Each team works in its workspace, using Save draft and Submit to director. They can resubmit until review starts.
  3. Review. Start review locks the teams out. A team that did not submit keeps the forecast and never blocks approval.
  4. Adjust or return. The director adjusts figures, rejects lines (this cannot be undone), or uses Return to teams, after which only the returned teams can edit.
  5. Approve. Freezes the lines and creates a demand set, with approved prices as overrides. Reopen goes back to review; approving again creates a new version, and earlier sets and plans stay.
1

  1. New cycle Directors and Admins. The start must be at least 7 days ahead, Cairo time. Admins can also allow an overlapping window or a past window.
  2. Next step For sales reps, a line saying what to do next.
  3. Window Dates, with Overlaps or Past window badges where they apply. The pencil and trash edit or delete Open cycles only; deleting removes saved lines.
  4. Status Open, In review, Returned or Approved, with how many teams submitted and who is outstanding.
  5. Open Reps get a button per team; directors get Review.

Commercial

Discounts & Rebates

A discount is a percentage off list price on a set of SKUs. A rebate is granted on every order in its window and clawed back if the customer misses the target. Nothing prices until the sales director approves it.

1
2
3
4
  1. New term One customer, many SKUs. A discount needs Discount %. A rebate needs Target basis, Target, Reward % and Valid to. All need Valid from and Reason. Save draft or Save & submit.
  2. Tabs Terms lists all terms by kind and state. Approvals is the director's queue and their default view; reps never see it. Rebate tracker shows approved rebates.
  3. Terms & window What was agreed, on which SKUs, and for how long.
  4. Approve or reject Directors can also End early, which is final and judges a rebate against a pro-rated target.

Rebate tracker shows progress, on-track state and clawback for each approved rebate. Record progress takes cumulative figures and a required note.

The rules it will follow

  1. You approve every change. It can read freely, but a run, cost what-if or saved-input change is only a proposal until you press Approve. Every proposal, approval and action is logged for Admins.
  2. Your role, nothing more. It sees only what you can see. Margin and price data stay limited to the roles allowed today.
  3. Every figure has a source. Answers cite the run, forecast or service behind each number, and unknowns are stated, never filled in. Questions outside planning are declined.
  4. Zero data retention. No customer, price or sales data is kept in a model provider, vector store or agent memory. Customer names are masked before the model sees them, and only an audit record outlives a session.

Day in the life

A typical day with Paula

A common planning routine takes 10–15 minutes, plus the time a run takes.

  1. Sign in with your own account. Planners land on Planning.
  2. Configure the run: choose the supply, Production or Demand, the period and prices. On a Demand run, upload a workbook, pick a saved set such as an approved commercial cycle, or use the model forecast.
  3. Optional per-run tweaks: Constraints, Categories, frozen storage dates, and the Allocate SKUs switch. These apply to this run only.
  4. Press Run Optimization. You are taken to the run's page. The floating pill follows you if you navigate away, and the bell records the result.
  5. Read the results tab by tab: Supply, Production (expand an option for each cut's yield, price factor and cost factor), Allocation, What if, Matrix, Per-day and Temperature. On a Demand run, add Demand and Demand uplift.
  6. Watch the alerts: plan warnings, a red "Period below fixed cost" banner, red-tinted breach days, "Cap-limited" badges, and coverage chips.
  7. Approve and share: Approve plan marks the run as the plan for its period, and Share run gives colleagues access. Export the full plan and the cutting instructions for the floor.
  8. Check where the kilograms go: SKU Allocation for customers and SKUs, Frozen recommendations for what to sell or hold.
  9. Sanity-check the market: Market Intelligence for predicted factors, the live-bird band and the demand forecast. Periodically, use Evaluation to confirm the models still deserve trust.

Sales teams and the director work on the cycle's own rhythm: teams submit, the director reviews and approves, and the approved demand set is ready for the next run.

Runs keep going if you close the page; reopen them from Run History or the bell. Loading can be slow on a cold start, so a view that spins for up to a minute and then recovers is normal.

Reference

Glossary

EGP
Egyptian pound. Appears on Market Intelligence, Commercial, Frozen recommendations, and in a few places on run results: direct cost per bird, and profit estimates for Admins and Planners. Most run figures are factors and indices.
Horizon
The planning window an optimization run covers — a range of working days.
Production mode
Default run type — the engine maximises profit from available supply, with no demand orders.
Demand mode
Run type that plans against a demand set: an uploaded workbook, a saved set, or the model forecast.
Demand set
The versioned demand a run serves. Saved with the run, so the run page always shows its own set, never the live forecast.
Light / Heavy bird
The two flock weight classes. Their split per day shows on the run's Supply and Per-day tabs.
Fresh
Product sold in the production month, not frozen.
Frozen
Product moved to cold storage — made in one month, delivered across the next. Frozen orders are owed by the month, not by any single day.
Strategy
How a day's output splits across fresh and frozen: All Fresh, All Frozen, or Mixed.
Temperature scope
The Mix | Fresh | Frozen filter on the run page's Production, Allocation, What if and Matrix tabs. While narrowed, figures are not run totals — a ribbon says so.
Dressed weight
A bird's weight after processing ≈ live weight × 0.73 (the plant's standing loss factor). Weight bands and distributions are in dressed grams.
Weight band
A dressed-weight range (e.g. 1,200–1,300 g) that output is allocated into — the grain of the Allocation tab.
Loss factor
Per-farm monthly rate of executed rejects plus mortality over total harvested — shown on the Supply tab.
Cutting option
A cutting recipe the engine can apply (e.g. Chest Deboning, Whole Chicken), defining how a carcass splits into products. Recipes are listed in Settings.
Packing
Whole-bird packing as a producer, allocated per weight band. Packing-only categories can never be deselected from a run.
Byproduct
A category that falls out of the cut regardless (e.g. offal) — deselecting it changes nothing, and uploaded byproduct demand is ignored.
Price factor
A category or SKU's predicted selling price as a multiple of the live-bird price — 2.40 means 2.4× the live-bird price that day.
Yield
The share of live weight that becomes a cut or a cutting option's output.
Cost factor
The cost side expressed the same way: cost ÷ live-bird price. Price factor minus cost factor is the margin per unit, the profit index.
Profit index
Price factor minus cost factor — dimensionless margin, comparable across categories.
Contribution index
The optimizer's ranking measure for a cutting option — how much a tonne through that option contributes. What-if rankings sort by it, not by output.
What-if scenario
A counterfactual: how every catalogue option would perform if the whole horizon (or one day) had run it — under Mixed, All Fresh, or All Frozen. The only actual tonnes on that tab are in "Chosen plan".
Fixed cost (FC)
The cost threshold the period must cover. The run page reports coverage as a percentage only — no EGP figure is shown.
Viability
A day's status against fixed cost on the Per-day tab; breached days tint red.
Cap-limited
A day or option clipped by a maximum constraint. A warning, not an error — exceeded soft caps are supported outcomes.
Constraint
A limit the optimizer must respect (birds, kg, or percentage; hard or soft; per day or per period). Durable constraints live in configuration; per-run overrides in the Planning form.
Degraded
A run recorded as completed whose result carries a caveat — shown amber in run lists; the "Completed" filter includes it.
Live bird price
The modelled farm-gate price per kg of live chicken that every price factor multiplies.
Model coverage
On Market Intelligence: the share of forecasts routed via the LightGBM model rather than a lookup fallback.
MAPE
Mean absolute percentage error — the Evaluation screen's headline accuracy measure. Lower is better.
Bias
The signed direction of forecast error — positive is over-forecasting, negative under-forecasting.
CV
Coefficient of variation — a unitless measure of how spread out the flock's weights are.
Commercial cycle
One submission window in which sales teams enter demand and prices and the sales director approves them, creating a demand set.
Nawlon
The transport component of the live bird cost: market price + nawlon + low-weight premium.
Rebate
A conditional discount, granted on every order in its window and clawed back if the customer misses the target.
Approved plan
The one run per period marked as the plan, using Approve plan.
Demand uplift
Extra demand for a blocking co-product that lets a cutting option run, recovering a short category.
Namespace
A group of related engine parameters on the Settings page (e.g. capacity, calendar, costs).
